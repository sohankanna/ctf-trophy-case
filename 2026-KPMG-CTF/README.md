# KPMG Cyber Threat Flag 2026: Campus Edition

- **Date:** October 9-11, 2026
- **Format:** Jeopardy-style, Individual CTF (48 hours)
- **Result:** 470 points across 27 solves
- **Writeups:** [22 challenge walkthroughs on my site](https://www.sohankanna.com/walkthroughs/series/kpmg-ctf-2026-campus-edition)

---

### Overview

KPMG India's Cyber Threat Flag is a 48-hour solo CTF aimed at campus talent. The board went well beyond the usual web and crypto categories. It covered **AI/LLM, API, Mobile, Incident Response, OSINT, Infrastructure, OT/IoT, Reverse Engineering, Cryptography and Web**, so it was a real test of breadth.

### Growth Since 2025

I played the 2025 edition too (105 points, 8 challenges). This year I finished with **470 points and 27 solves**, more than four times the score. That gap is the clearest measure in this repo of how much I've improved in a year.

### What I Solved

-   **AI/LLM:** Cleared all three: SecureBot Support, AcmeBank Assistant and WebSummarizer Pro.
-   **API:** TradeBot, Arclight 1 and NexusAPI (3 of 4).
-   **Incident Response:** Operation Dead Drop and Six Ways Out.
-   **OSINT:** Cleared all three: A Clean Trail, Westbound and Golden Hour.
-   **Mobile:** Latitude of Injection.
-   **Infrastructure, OT/IoT, Reverse Engineering, Crypto and Web:** Healthy Hijack 1, Locks of Confusion 1, VaultDocs 1 & 2, Baud Intentions, Last Will, Logic Bypass, Algebraic Nightmare, Matrix Reload, Onion, Corrupted Vision and DevNote.

### Key Takeaways

-   **AI security is now a real CTF category:** Every LLM challenge was about getting an assistant to break its own rules. That kind of bug is showing up in real products right now.
-   **Breadth beats depth in a 48-hour solo event:** With ten categories and nobody to split work with, picking up easier solves across the whole board mattered more than grinding one hard challenge.
-   **Writing it up:** I published walkthroughs for 22 of the challenges. Explaining a solve forces you to understand exactly why it worked.
